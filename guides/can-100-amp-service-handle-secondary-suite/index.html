Can a 100 Amp Service Handle a Secondary Suite?

Real World Electric · Updated 3 November 2026

Start with the actual loads

A second kitchen does not automatically require a 200 A service. Equally, a 100 A main breaker does not prove that a proposed suite will fit. Calculate the demand for the complete project and check the existing service.

Connected load and calculated demand differ

CEC dwelling rules apply demand factors to specified loads. Multi-unit calculations also have particular rules for combining dwellings. These allowances do not apply to every load, and they do not replace a check of the service equipment.

Equipment that can change the answer

LoadWhat to record
Electric heating and air conditioningEquipment ratings and whether loads can operate together.
RangesThe range rating in each dwelling, including the proposed suite.
Electric water heatingWhether each heater is storage-tank or tankless, and its nameplate rating.
EV chargingThe EVSE rating and any proposed energy management system.
Pool, hot tub or spaThe equipment nameplate and any planned additions.

There is no reliable rule that two particular appliances will overload a service. Their ratings and the other loads matter. Include planned equipment in the same calculation instead of assessing additions separately.

If the calculated demand is too high

1. Review equipment choices

Compare different equipment ratings and heating, cooking or water-heating options. Recalculate the complete project for each option. Installation cost, available utilities and the household's needs also matter.

2. Assess load management

Where EV charging is involved, an EVEMS may allow charging to be reduced or paused as other loads rise. Its treatment depends on the equipment and installation. The calculator does not model EVEMS operation; have a proposed system assessed separately.

3. Plan a service upgrade

If the proposed loads cannot be accommodated, confirm the required service size and utility capacity. In Calgary, a service change involves the City and ENMAX. Compare a project-specific upgrade quote with the alternatives.

Use a checked calculation when comparing quotes

Ask which loads and assumptions support the proposed service size. A calculation can help explain the decision, but an online estimate alone does not establish that the installed service is suitable.

What about a 60 A service?

The Calgary worksheet lists a minimum of 100 A for a single dwelling with at least 80 m² excluding basement, and 60 A below that area. Do not treat those figures as a complete ruling on an existing installation or a new suite. Have the proposed arrangement assessed before budgeting for either retention or replacement.

Frequently asked questions

Can a 100 amp service handle a secondary suite?

It may. Check the combined calculated demand, the existing service and the proposed equipment. A second dwelling does not by itself establish the required service size.

Which loads should I check first?

Record heating and cooling, ranges in both dwellings, water heaters, EV charging, and any pool, hot tub or spa equipment. Their actual ratings matter more than a general ranking of appliances.

Can load management avoid a service upgrade?

A suitable EVEMS may help where EV charging adds demand. Have its equipment acceptance, controls and installation requirements assessed separately; the calculator does not model EVEMS operation.

Is 100 A always the minimum for a house with a suite?

The City's worksheet gives single-dwelling minimums based on floor area excluding basement. The applicable requirements for the existing service and proposed suite arrangement need a project-specific assessment.
