Planning the electrical work for a home renovation

Real World Electric · Updated 17 November 2026

What to settle before buying equipment

A lighting purchase, appliance choice or cabinet change can affect work elsewhere in a renovation. If a problem emerges after installation or during an inspection, correcting it may mean replacing equipment, altering work or reopening finished areas. An early conversation can help identify overlooked requirements before those decisions become expensive to reverse.

The initial consultation is a 60-minute video call for $150 CAD with a Master Electrician. Bring your basement, kitchen, lighting or other home project and the questions holding it up. Use the hour to review your priorities, identify missing information and decide what needs attention next.

What to cover in an electrical planning call

Choose the topics that matter to your project. Bring plans and specifications so we can focus the hour and identify any follow-up work.

Equipment approvals and part numbers

Check the approval markings required for the equipment and proposed installation before buying. For plug-in electrical products, Health Canada identifies Canadian certification marks such as CSA, cUL and cETL. Its electrical product safety guidance explains these requirements and links to the recognized marks list.

Bring the exact model and part numbers, label photos you already have, and manufacturer instructions. We can discuss whether a product's intended use and installation conditions fit the proposal, which accessories belong with it, and what still needs confirmation before purchase.

Code questions and commonly missed details

A marked-up plan makes a code question easier to discuss. Identify what exists, what is changing and the detail you are unsure about. Typical planning issues include overlooked fixed appliances, cabinet changes that affect outlet locations, incompatible components, and arranging inspections before work is concealed. The aim is to find the unresolved decisions while there is still room to change the plan.

Lighting, dimmers and automation

Review the intended use of each room, useful light output, colour quality, glare and control locations. Compare fixture pricing with the features, installation needs and replacement options that affect its value. Bring product links or quotes to discuss the differences. We can also work through compatibility questions between lighting, dimmers and automation controls, including how you want the system to behave during everyday use.

Appliances, service capacity and EV charging

Bring the ratings for new ranges, electric heat and EV chargers so the electrician can review them together. If you need to check a charger against your existing service, request an EV charger load assessment. A signed electrical load calculation is a separate service.

HVAC controls, floor heating and steam units

Bring the model details and manufacturer documents for HVAC controls, pressure switches, in-floor heating or steam units. We can discuss the equipment's electrical supply and control requirements, how it relates to other parts of the project, and which questions belong with an electrician, HVAC technician or manufacturer. This is a planning discussion; adjustments, troubleshooting and installation that require qualified on-site work need a separate arrangement.

Basements and laneway houses

Start with the proposed rooms, their intended use and the equipment serving them. A basement renovation, secondary suite and laneway house can involve different supply arrangements and approval questions. We can identify what your drawings and equipment list need to show, which capacity questions to investigate, and what to confirm with the municipality before construction. The type of project does not by itself establish eligibility for a homeowner permit.

Smoke and CO alarms, cameras and doorbells

Discuss the alarm types, locations and interconnection requirements that need confirmation for the proposed layout. For cameras, video doorbells and CCTV, bring your coverage goals, product choices and network plan. Reviewing power, connectivity and cable-route questions before finishes are installed can help coordinate the systems with the rest of the work.

Confirm what you are allowed to do

In Calgary, homeowner permit eligibility generally requires legal ownership, living in the home or intending to live there, and doing the work yourself. It does not cover rental properties or a contractor working under your homeowner permit. Apartment-style condominiums are excluded.

Some work also falls outside homeowner permit scope, including changes to main service equipment, solar installations and EV charging wiring. These are examples, not a complete list. Check the City's homeowner permit eligibility and work restrictions before planning the work. Outside Calgary, confirm requirements with your local permitting authority.

What to bring to your planning call

  • A floor plan showing the rooms, furniture, cabinets and proposed changes.
  • Photos taken from a safe position, without removing electrical covers.
  • Product specifications, part numbers, quotes and your shopping list.
  • Your permit status, project stage and any written inspection comments.
  • The questions you most want answered during the hour.

Video and photos have limits; concealed conditions or uncertain equipment details may need an on-site assessment. The call is for discussion and review, not energized work.

When a follow-up planning call can help

An initial planning call can be followed by a separate consultation at rough-in or before final inspection. Bring updated photos and any changes to the plan, so the discussion reflects the project as it stands.

Calgary requires at least rough-in and final inspections for homeowner electrical permits. Plan the required inspections before concealing work, following the City's electrical wiring guide, page 4. A consultation is separate from those inspections, engineering and any required installation or sign-off service.

Frequently asked questions

What does the consultation cost?

The initial consultation is $150 CAD for a 60-minute video call with a Master Electrician. Bring your plans, product details and questions so the time focuses on your project.

Are follow-up calls and messaging included?

Follow-up calls are optional, separate bookings. Project messaging is scoped separately; it is not unlimited support included with the initial call.

Does a video consultation replace an inspection?

No. A consultation does not replace required municipal inspections, authorize work or certify an installation. Decisions about permit acceptance remain with the authority having jurisdiction.

Does this include a signed load calculation?

No. A consultation can identify whether your project needs a load calculation. Calculation review and sign-off are a separate service with their own scope and fee.
