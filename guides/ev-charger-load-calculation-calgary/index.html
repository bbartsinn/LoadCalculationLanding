EV Charger Load Calculation in Calgary: Do You Need a Service Upgrade?

Real World Electric · Updated 3 November 2026

Start with the charger rating

Unmanaged EV charging is counted at its full equipment load in the dwelling calculation. Use the EVSE rating rather than the branch-circuit breaker rating, and record the units correctly.

Amps and watts are different inputs

A 48 A charger operating at 240 V represents 11,520 W. Its 60 A branch-circuit breaker is a different rating. If the calculator asks for watts, enter the equipment load in watts and verify the supply assumptions.

Compare charging needs with the vehicle's capability, daily travel and available charging time. A higher-rated charger adds more load; it is not automatically necessary for every household.

Check the whole project

Include the dwelling areas, heating and cooling, ranges, water heaters and other applicable fixed loads. Include a planned suite or hot tub in the same assessment. The service needs to support the proposed combination, not just each addition considered separately.

Demand factors apply to specified loads under the dwelling rules. Do not apply a general household discount to the EVSE rating. For the suite side, see whether a 100 A service can support a secondary suite.

When an EVEMS may help

An electric vehicle energy management system can reduce or pause charging in response to other loads. Whether it allows the existing service to be retained depends on the equipment, controls and installation.

Check the City's current EMS/EVEMS equipment and permit guidance. Accepted equipment and other arrangements can have different submission requirements. Charging time also depends on how much capacity remains available; a full charge by morning is not guaranteed.

Calculator scope

This calculator accepts EV charging load in watts. It does not model EVEMS operation or check equipment acceptance. Have a managed-charging proposal assessed separately instead of entering an arbitrary reduced EV load.

Compare the available options

1. Charging without load management

Have the proposed charger rating checked against the calculated demand and the existing service. Capacity is one part of the assessment; the branch circuit, equipment and permit requirements still apply.

2. A different charger rating or EVEMS

Compare an appropriately configured lower charging rate with a suitable EVEMS. Confirm the equipment settings and installation requirements, and ask for a quote covering the complete work.

3. A service upgrade

If the proposed loads cannot be accommodated, review the service size and utility capacity. In Calgary, coordinate a service change with ENMAX and the City. Confirm the scope and lead time before ordering equipment.

Permit and installation requirements

Use the City's electrical permit guidance to confirm the application and inspection requirements, who can undertake the work, and what information must be submitted. A calculator result does not authorise an installation or confirm that a particular service, panel or charger arrangement is suitable.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a service upgrade for an EV charger in Calgary?

Not necessarily. Have the existing service and proposed loads assessed. Charger rating, suitable load management and the condition and ratings of the service equipment can affect the options.

Can a 100 amp service support a Level 2 charger?

It may, depending on the other loads and the proposed charger rating. A 100 A main breaker alone does not establish that there is enough capacity.

Can the calculator assess an EVEMS installation?

No. It accepts EV charging watts but does not model energy management or check equipment acceptance. A proposed EVEMS needs a separate assessment.

Do I need an electrical permit for an EV charger?

Calgary identifies an electrical permit for EV charging. Check the current City guidance for application eligibility, inspections and any EMS/EVEMS submission requirements.

Planning information only. Confirm the calculation, equipment and current submission requirements for your project. Final acceptance rests with the authority having jurisdiction.
