Electrical questions the call can cover

Code & equipment approval

Discuss the code rules that apply to your project, common requirements and pitfalls. Review Canadian equipment approval markings, part numbers and whether a product is suitable for the proposed installation.

Lighting & automation

Compare lighting quality, price and value for your rooms. Work through fixture choices, dimmer compatibility, smart switches and automation so the controls and lighting work together as intended.

Power, appliances & EVs

Review appliance power requirements, load calculations and service-upgrade questions. For a review of the capacity available for a specific charger, request an EV charger load assessment.

Heating & controls

Bring questions about HVAC controls, pressure switches, in-floor heating and steam units. Review equipment documentation, electrical requirements and how the controls need to coordinate with the rest of the project.

Basements & laneway homes

Plan the electrical side of a basement development, kitchen renovation or laneway house. Discuss layouts, future needs, trade coordination and the details to resolve before purchases, rough-in and inspections.

Alarms, cameras & security

Discuss smoke and carbon monoxide alarms, camera systems, doorbell cameras and CCTV. Review product choices, power and connection needs, and the project requirements that affect the plan.

From planning to completion

When to arrange a planning call

Start with the whole project, or bring a specific question. Further calls can focus on the stage you have reached.

  1. Before you begin

    Bring a sketch, product links and your priorities. Discuss scope, materials, permit eligibility and the questions to resolve before starting.

  2. At rough-in

    Arrange a follow-up discussion while work is still visible. Review questions about your progress and prepare for the required municipal inspection.

  3. Before final inspection

    Talk through outstanding questions and finishing details. Your municipality or inspection authority makes the inspection and acceptance decisions.

The $150 fee covers one 60-minute call. Additional calls are separate bookings. If you need messaging between calls, agree on the scope and fee first.

What to bring to the call

Share whichever of these details you have.

  • Your project location and a short description of the work
  • A floor-plan sketch and photos you already have
  • Product links, model numbers, equipment labels or a materials list
  • Your main questions and any written inspection comments

Calgary homeowner permits and inspections

Homeowner electrical permits have limits. In Calgary, eligibility depends on who owns and occupies the home and the work proposed. Changes to main service equipment, solar, battery storage and EV charging are among the work excluded from homeowner electrical permits.

A consultation helps you plan and ask better questions. It does not replace permits, required inspections, an on-site assessment or contractor work where required. A video call cannot verify concealed work or certify the installation.
